This paper documents the development and validation of the **Zero-Point Protocol (ZPP)** — a framework for evaluating and preserving semantic integrity in large language model (LLM) interactions.

The work emerged from a **one-year longitudinal observation period spanning 10,000+ conversational turns** across multiple frontier models, including ChatGPT, Claude, Grok, Gemini, DeepSeek, and Meta AI. 

During early exploratory phases, repeated observations included substantial instability: 

- _Semantic drift_
    
- _Behavioral inconsistency_
    
- _Post-alignment response variance_
    
- _Refusal discontinuities_
    
- High variance in model framing under similar inputs

These observations were treated as **hypothesis-generating conditions rather than formal evidence**. The longitudinal period informed the development of a structured methodology designed to transform exploratory observations into measurable, reproducible tests.

The resulting framework evolved through four stages:

| Phase                                | Focus                                               | Outcome                                                                                                      |
| ------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Phase 1 — Observation**            | Longitudinal multi-model interaction                | Identification of recurring semantic instability patterns                                                    |
| **Phase 2 — Instrumentation**        | Development of deterministic scoring and routing    | Creation of the Zero-Point Protocol                                                                          |
| **Phase 3 — Distributed Validation** | Multi-node cryptographic consensus and auditability | Live multi-port TCP mesh network execution, automated process orchestration, and asynchronous fork isolation |
| **Phase 4 — Legible Divergence**     | Behavioral comparison under isolated conditions     | Framework for stable differentiation analysis                                                                |

These observations and implementation stages produced both concrete protocol components and broader architectural findings.

### Core Finding

This work demonstrates that post-training alignment layers introduce observable semantic variance, and proves that this variance can be programmatically audited, scored, and routed through a deterministic multi-node architecture using localized threshold calibration and decentralized transport networks.
